\section{Scope}
This supplement expands Section~V of the conference paper.

\section{Why Salinas-A under SWIR-only reaches paired ARI 0.766}

Salinas-A is a 6-class subset of Salinas covering five lettuce
varieties (4-, 5-, 6-, 7-week) plus a single soil class on an
$83 \times 86$ window. The SWIR window (1100--2500~nm) carries the
cellulose, lignin, and water-absorption features that historically
dominate vegetation--soil discrimination on AVIRIS. Two specific
observations support the high paired ARI on this tuple:

\paragraph{(i) Cellulose feature density.} The SWIR window contains
the canonical cellulose absorption near 2100~nm and the
lignin/glucose absorption near 2300~nm; together these are
sufficient to discriminate vegetation life stage on the
lettuce-romaine sequence at the centre of Salinas-A. The unmasked
canonical fit picks up the same features (it has access to them) and
the SWIR-only fit picks up exactly those features again --- the
topic identities are conserved because the discriminative signal
literally lives in the retained spectral range.

\paragraph{(ii) Spatial-class smoothness.} Salinas-A is, by design,
the spatially smoothest of the six labelled scenes. Each class
occupies a contiguous rectangular field with sharp boundaries; the
canonical and masked dominant-topic maps agree on the field
interiors and disagree mostly at the field boundaries, which
contribute a bounded swap-rate floor independent of band restriction.

The SWIR-only paired ARI on Salinas (the parent scene) does
\emph{not} reach 0.766 --- it is $0.435$, lower than the VNIR and
no-water masks. This is consistent with mechanism (i) operating on
Salinas-A's narrower class set but failing on the parent's broader
vinyard and brocoli classes whose discriminative signal lives
partly in VNIR.

\section{Why KSC and Botswana reach paired ARI \texorpdfstring{$\approx 0.01$}{~0.01}}

KSC paired ARI $\approx 0.01$ across all four masks; Botswana
$\approx 0.01$--$0.13$. Two non-exclusive mechanisms are consistent
with this finding:

\paragraph{Mechanism (i): canonical fit exploits band-level
redundancy.} The unmasked LDA fit on KSC may be using fine-grained
spectral co-variation that is present in the full $176$-band
representation and disappears under restriction. Under this
mechanism the canonical basis is correct in the unmasked reference
frame but is \emph{not} transportable across spectral windows. Any
claim of the form ``topic~$k$ corresponds to a wetland-vegetation
type'' must be qualified: it presupposes the unmasked spectrum.

\paragraph{Mechanism (ii): weak identification.} KSC has 13 classes,
$D = 2686$ in the canonical sample, and $B = 176$, giving a
posterior over $\phi$ with $\sim 2100$ parameters and $\sim 2700$
documents. The posterior has substantial mass over many
high-likelihood configurations, and a band restriction selects a
different posterior mode of the same family. The masked fit is not
\emph{wrong}, it is merely a different sample from a weakly
informative posterior.

\textbf{Discriminating between (i) and (ii) is out of scope of the
diagnostic.} A natural follow-up is a posterior-mode-counting
diagnostic (refit under many seeds and cluster the resulting
$\{\phi^{(s)}\}$ for $s = 1, \ldots, N$ seeds; count distinct
clusters above a cosine-similarity threshold). The companion code
repository already ships such a diagnostic under \texttt{topic\_stability/}
and \texttt{deep\_seed\_stability/} but its interpretation is outside
this conference paper's scope.

\section{Reading the ARI-vs-label column}

A subtle but important point. The ARI-vs-label column in
Suppl D Table I reports
$\ari(\text{masked dominant topic}, \text{ground-truth label})$. A
high value means the masked fit is still discriminating the
ground-truth classes even though its topic identities have changed.
The clearest example is Pavia~U $\times$ VNIR with paired ARI
$=0.415$ and ARI-vs-label $=0.316$: the masked fit is still useful
for downstream classification (it preserves about as much label
discrimination as the canonical fit, $\ari \approx 0.32$) even
though the topic identities have shifted. Conversely, Botswana
$\times$ SWIR has paired ARI $=0.013$ \emph{and} ARI-vs-label
$=0.098$: the masked fit is both topically unstable and operationally
weaker.

\section{What the diagnostic does NOT claim}

We close with four explicit non-claims:

\paragraph{It does not claim a single best band selection.} The
top-50 Fisher mask achieves the lowest paired-ARI on Indian Pines
($0.398$) and the highest on Pavia~U ($0.387$), so the same
``50-band-budget'' mask is interpretable differently on different
scenes. Selecting a band budget for a downstream product is a
separate optimisation that should marginalise over the per-scene
diagnostic.

\paragraph{It does not claim a chance-baseline threshold.}
Statistical significance of paired ARI under a permutation null is
not reported here; the threshold for ``meaningful agreement'' is a
matter of judgement on the present sample sizes ($D = 1320$--$3520$).
A formal permutation test is in the companion code repository under
\texttt{validation\_blocks/}.

\paragraph{It does not claim that band-fragility implies model
inadequacy.} A scene whose canonical topics are band-fragile may
still admit a useful interpretation under a different inductive bias
(e.g.\ deep autoencoder embedding); the diagnostic asks specifically
whether the \emph{LDA} basis is band-robust, not whether the data
admits any band-robust representation.

\paragraph{It does not claim transferability to non-AVIRIS sensors
without recalibration.} The band-mask wavelengths are read from the
per-scene \texttt{wavelengths\_nm} metadata; running the diagnostic
on a sensor with different band centres requires recomputing the
mask membership functions but no algorithmic change.
