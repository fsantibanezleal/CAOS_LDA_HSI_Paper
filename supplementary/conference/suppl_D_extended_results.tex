\section{Reading the swap-rate column}

The swap rate under Hungarian alignment is, by definition,
$\ge 0$ and $\le 1 - 1/K$ (a perfectly random permutation under $K$
balanced clusters reaches a swap rate of $1 - 1/K$). On the labelled
$K=12$ scenes the upper bound is $\sim 0.917$. The values observed:

\begin{itemize}
\item Indian Pines: swap rates $0.45$--$0.53$ across masks; the
canonical basis is partially robust.
\item Salinas: swap rates $0.35$--$0.44$; similar to Indian Pines.
\item Salinas-A: swap rate as low as $0.18$ under SWIR; this is the
strongest single recovery in the entire diagnostic.
\item KSC: swap rates $0.53$--$0.64$; band-fragile across masks.
\item Botswana: swap rates $0.64$--$0.75$; the most extreme
band-fragile scene in the diagnostic.
\end{itemize}

Note that the swap rate and the paired ARI agree on the
band-fragile/band-robust split: scenes with low paired ARI also have
high swap rate. The two metrics are complementary, however:
ARI corrects for chance given the marginal sizes, swap rate is a
direct fraction of mis-assigned pixels, and the two diverge when one
fit's marginal is strongly imbalanced (e.g.\ Salinas-A SWIR's
prevalence $[0.188, 0.124, 0.094, 0.365, 0.187, 0.043]$ is closer
to balanced than KSC's, so the chance correction is smaller, so the
ARI value is closer to the swap-rate complement).
